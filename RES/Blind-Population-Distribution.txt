Distribution of Our U.S. Blind Population

Data as of May 29, 2025, at 2:30 PM Eastern time.

This text gives the same numbers as the chart. The chart shows how many blind people in the United States have each kind of extra disability, and how many blind people there are in the United States and in the world.

The groups overlap. One person can be in more than one group. So the percents add to more than 100. The numbers are estimates.

Blind people in the United States, by group

Blindness only, no major extra disability: 50 percent, about 500,000 people.
Mobility impairment: 22.5 percent, about 225,000 people.
Intellectual or developmental disability: 20 percent, about 200,000 people.
Multiple disabilities, two or more extra disabilities: 15 percent, about 150,000 people.
Traumatic brain injury: 5 percent, about 50,000 people.
Deafblind, hearing loss and vision loss: 4.5 percent, about 45,000 people.
Autism spectrum disorder: 4 percent, about 40,000 people.
Cerebral palsy: 3.5 percent, about 35,000 people.
At least one category, all blind people: 100 percent, about 1,000,000 people.

Blind people in the world

United States: about 1,000,000 people.
Rest of the world: about 42,000,000 people.
World total: about 43,000,000 people.

Sources: data.gov, the World Health Organization (who.int), and national blind groups. Made by Gregory AB9MZ from public data.
